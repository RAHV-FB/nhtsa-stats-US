01 / THE EVIDENCE

The patterns are less simple than the headlines.

Counting a behaviour in a fatal crash is not the same as showing that it caused the death. These findings separate raw frequency, conditional severity, confounding, and evidence quality.

01 — CRASH SEVERITY

Being common in crashes does not mean being common in fatal crashes.

Pedestrian-involved crashes make up roughly 1.2% of reported crashes but 18.6% of fatal ones. Compare the frequency of a circumstance with its over-representation among fatalities.

03 / MULTIPLE FACTORS

One death can appear in several categories.

Official factor counts overlap. They cannot be added into a breakdown of “causes”, and statistical attribution changes depending on the question being asked.

65%Deaths involving at least one listed factor

Across 2016–2024, the four counts refer to speeding, BAC ≥ 0.08, drug-positive drivers and unbelted occupants. The same death can appear more than once. Drug-positive counts are lower bounds given missing tests.

04 / POLICY LIMITS

What would an intervention have to achieve?

These data do not measure speed-camera programmes, roadside checks or other enforcement interventions. We can still calculate transparent break-even requirements without inventing treatment effects.

WHAT THIS DOES ↘

Arithmetic, not a prediction.

The calculator divides the desired national reduction by the observed deaths in a targeted segment. It does not assume how many drivers change behaviour or how effective enforcement is.

  • Target segments overlap; adding them double-counts deaths.
  • No exposure, intervention placement or enforcement cost data exist in the archives.
  • Risk ratios conditional on crashes cannot be treated as causal effects.
  • Drug-positive counts understate involvement where testing is missing.

06 / METHODS & TRANSPARENCY

Built to be challenged.

The project combines a census of fatal crashes with a probability sample of police-reported crashes. Its key limitations shape what the findings actually mean.

01

Two different data systems

FARS counts fatal crashes; CRSS samples police-reported crashes across severities. Survey-weighted denominators and design-based confidence intervals are essential.

02

Conditional on a reported crash

No trip or distance-exposure data are available. A fatality risk ratio conditional on a crash does not tell us the probability of crashing per trip or kilometre.

03

Association, not causation

Crash configuration, road environment, reporting differences and missingness can alter an association. Adjusted results are not intervention effect estimates.

04

Reproducible and qualified

Source archives, validation checks, generated tables, report templates and tests are held in the public repository. Estimates distinguish observed, estimated, adjusted, predicted and hypothetical results.
