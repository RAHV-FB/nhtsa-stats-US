# Audit and re-analysis, November 2026

The owner's review of the public site (PR #6, "plain data stories") was that it showed generic
statistics of little value, and asked for a full audit and a re-analysis that does not start from
the project's existing framing. This document records what was checked, what was found and what
changed. Work started from the branch of PR #6 (`cl/ecstatic-hamilton-mnoqzr`), which contains
`main` plus the PR #6 site.

## 3. Findings of the audit

1. **The numbers were right; the questions and the presentation were not.** The research was
   organised around one question: given a police-reported crash, which circumstances make it more
   likely to be fatal. That is a sound question, but the public site turned it into either a
   methodology-first "debunking" page (PR #5) or generic descriptive counts (PR #6: deaths by year,
   time of day, age). Neither said which behaviours, people, vehicles and places account for the
   deaths, which is what a reader needs first.
2. **Important results existed but were not surfaced.** The adjusted risk of death for occupants
   of old vehicles (`person/lpv_killed_crude_vs_adjusted`), the rider–passenger helmet comparison
   and the belt comparison (`matched/`) were in the tables but nowhere on the site.
3. **Questions the data can answer had not been asked:** who the victims of drunk and speeding
   drivers are; the licence status and prior convictions of the drivers involved; how states
   differ; the changing age of the vehicles people die in; which groups drove the 2020–21 rise and
   which have not come back down.
4. **One overlap measure used a factor the project itself says cannot be compared.**
   `contributions/fars_overlap_summary` puts "driver drug-positive (determinate crashes only)" into
   the union of four factors, although drug testing is selective and differs by state
   (`docs/limitations.md` §2 and §7). The new overlap (`deaths/behaviour_summary`) uses drink-driving,
   speeding, unbelted and unhelmeted victims. The Phase-2 table is left in place (it is a validated
   output and its report describes it correctly), but it is no longer used on the site.
5. **Belt effectiveness depends on recording accuracy.** The within-vehicle belt estimate is 66% as
   recorded and falls quickly if surviving occupants' belt use is misrecorded
   (`matched/belt_effect`). Any use of it, including the new lives-saved figures, shows the
   scenarios.
6. **Site engineering.** The PR #6 page typed numbers into HTML and checked them with a regular
   expression test, drew charts in the browser (blank "Loading chart…" boxes without JavaScript)
   and re-rendered Markdown reports in the page. The PR #5 page also exposed a policy calculator
   whose outputs come from user assumptions rather than data.

## 4. What changed

* **New analysis** `src/nhtsa_stats/analysis/deaths.py` (`nhtsa analyze deaths`, a few seconds):
  13 tables in `outputs/tables/deaths/` with provenance sidecars; methods in
  `docs/methodology.md` §13, limitations in `docs/limitations.md` §8; tests in
  `tests/test_deaths.py`.
* **New report** `reports/road_deaths.md`, rendered from `reports/templates/road_deaths.md`.
* **New public site**, rendered from `website/templates/index.html` by `nhtsa analyze site`, so every
  number is looked up in `outputs/tables` at build time; charts are drawn at build time from the same
  tables; `tests/test_site.py` fails on a stale page or on a number in the template that is not a
  placeholder.
* **Unchanged:** every Phase-1 and Phase-2 module, table, report and documented limitation; the
  Streamlit app.
