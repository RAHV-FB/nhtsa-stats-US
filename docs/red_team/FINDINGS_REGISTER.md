# Register of the major published findings

Each finding is quoted as published, with numbers as rendered in the reports. "Published" covers:

* the README "Findings" and "Phase 2 findings" lists;
* the summaries of `reports/*.md`;
* the "Key findings" list and section headlines of the public website
  (`website/templates/index.html`).

## Phase 1: what makes a police-reported crash deadly (`reports/findings.md`)

| ID | Finding as published | Where | Tables | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | **Frequency is not danger.** Pedestrian crashes are 1.2% of police-reported crashes but 18.6% of fatal ones (over-representation ×15.6, 13.2–18.5). Exceeding the limit ×12.8, motorcycles ×9.5. Rain, wet roads, intersections and teen drivers are common in crashes but under-represented among fatal ones. CRSS denominators are combined with FARS numerators. | findings.md §2; README finding 1 | `descriptive/frequency_vs_severity`, `measurement/fars_crss_fatal_composition` | `analysis/descriptive.py`, `analysis/measurement.py` |
| F02 | **Adjustment changes the picture.** Night 00–06h crude RR 3.97 becomes 1.62 (model A). Shapley shares of the night excess: light 32%, alcohol 20%. Rain crude 0.71 becomes 1.00 once the surface is held fixed. Interstates go from 1.36 crude to 0.70 adjusted. Driver 65+ goes from 1.13 to 1.66 ("unmasked"). Teen drivers stay below 1 (0.86). | findings.md §3; README 2 | `severity/fatal_crude_vs_adjusted`, `severity/ka_crude_vs_adjusted`, `confounding/*` | `analysis/severity.py`, `analysis/confounding.py`, `analysis/specs.py` |
| F03 | **"Speeding" is two things.** Exceeded the limit or racing: adjusted fatal RR 5.22 (4.34–6.27). "Too fast for conditions" (three-quarters of speeding-related crashes): 1.06 (0.89–1.26), "no detectable excess" (KA 1.18). Under a QBA the exceedance association falls below 1 only if non-fatal exceedance is recorded about a tenth as completely as fatal. | findings.md §4; README 3; website #speed | `severity/fatal_speeding_type`, `severity/ka_speeding_type`, `measurement/qba_grid`, `burden/attributable_fractions_fatal` | `analysis/severity.py`, `analysis/measurement.py` |
| F04 | **The most robust associations involve objective circumstances.** Adjusted RRs: motorcycle 8.6 (7.8–9.6); 50–55 mph 2.44 and 70+ mph 2.30 vs 30–35; unlit dark 1.74; heavy truck 1.91. Alcohol is large under every coding: 4.2 imputed, 5.9 complete-case, 2.8 with unknown counted as no. Struck pedestrian: RR of death 6.6 (4.8–9.0) at 50–55 vs ≤25 mph; SUV 1.25 and pickup 1.32 vs car. | findings.md §5; README 4 | `severity/fatal_crude_vs_adjusted`, `measurement/alcohol_coding_sensitivity`, `vulnerable/pedestrian_killed_contrasts`, `vulnerable/pedestrian_light_truck_by_speed` | `analysis/severity.py`, `analysis/measurement.py`, `analysis/vulnerable.py` |
| F05 | **Some combinations amplify each other.** Pedestrian × unlit darkness amplifies on both scales (additive IC 93.6 per 1,000; ratio of RRs 1.62). Motorcycle × alcohol is additive-super but sub-multiplicative. Speeding × darkness (RRR 0.94) and young driver × night (1.19) are "not detected". The protective speeding × wet interaction is explained by the mix of speeding types. KA: speeding × unlit has an additive excess. | findings.md §6; reports/interactions.md; README 5 | `interactions/pairs_fatal`, `pairs_ka`, `pairs_fatal_by_period`, `triples_fatal` | `analysis/interactions.py` |
| F06 | **Involvement is not severity (quasi-induced exposure).** Teen drivers are over-represented as the culpable party (RIR 1.92, 1.87–1.97) but their crashes are less severe (0.86). Drivers 75+ have RIR 1.65. The alcohol RIR falls from 6.5 to 2.5 across the coding break, an artefact. | findings.md §7; README 6 | `exposure/relative_involvement`, `exposure/relative_involvement_ka`, `exposure/relative_involvement_charge_based`, `exposure/sample` | `analysis/exposure.py` |
| F07 | **Belts, within the same vehicle.** In FARS double-pair comparisons, belted front-seat occupants' risk of death was 65.8% (64.5–67.0%) lower; conditional-logit OR 5.07. The estimate falls to 37.9% if 10% of "belted" survivors were misrecorded, 4.4% at 20%, and vanishes at about 21%. Belted 65+ vs 25–44: 2.81× the risk. Helmets: RR 0.69. Across crashes (CRSS) the unbelted RR is 46 crude and 24 adjusted, "not belt effectiveness". | findings.md §8; README 7 | `matched/*`, `person/lpv_killed_*` | `analysis/matched.py`, `analysis/person.py` |
| F08 | **The post-2020 rise.** The fatal share rose from 0.51% to 0.64% (RR 1.25). Fewer reported crashes account for 54% (29–78%) of the rise. Recorded composition accounts for 28–49% (reference-dependent). Within the same circumstances the share is 1.11× higher, clearest for speeding (1.38). The 2023–24 excess depends on the outcome definition. | findings.md §9; README 8 | `trends/*` | `analysis/trends.py` |
| F09 | **Measurement.** Police-recorded behaviours are recorded more completely in severe crashes. Alcohol testing: 74% of killed vs 28% of surviving drivers. The CRSS alcohol unknown share jumped from 11% to 30% in 2019. Reported belt use is 98.7% among uninjured vs 50.8% among killed. Drug and distraction fields "cannot support severity or trend analysis". | findings.md §10; README 9 | `measurement/*` | `analysis/measurement.py` |
| F10 | **Burden and interventions.** Severity-pathway attributable fractions: speeding 9.1% of fatal crashes (≈3,736 deaths/yr), alcohol 19.7% (≈8,072/yr), joint 26.5%, exceedance 5.8%, too-fast 0.5%. The speeding fraction shrinks towards zero under under-recording; the alcohol fraction does not. The data identify no intervention effect. | findings.md §11; README 10; reports/policy_analysis.md | `burden/*`, `policy/evidence_ledger`, `simulation/*` | `analysis/burden.py`, `simulation/` |
| F11 | **Prediction models.** Temporal split: train 2016–21, tune 2022, test 2023–24. Boosted AUROC 0.921 (0.912–0.929) with configuration vs 0.903 logistic; pre-crash only 0.835. AP 0.165 at base rate 0.006. Calibration O/P 1.04 overall but 0.47 in the lowest-risk half. Drift 2020–22. Two ML-screened interactions confirmed on the test years (intersection × head-on 0.17; speeding × angle 2.11). "Inputs exclude anything recorded after the crash … enforced by the same leakage gate." | findings.md §12; docs/model_validation.md | `ml/*` | `models/severity_ml.py`, `processing/harmonize.py` |

## Phase 2: overlapping factors (`reports/factor_contributions.md`, `speeding_confounding.md`, `alcohol_drugs_restraints.md`, `multifactor_interactions.md`, `case_comparisons.md`)

| ID | Finding as published | Where | Tables | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P01 | **Official factor counts overlap and cannot be added.** Deaths in speeding-related crashes (98,693), with a driver at BAC ≥ .08 (105,821), with a drug-positive driver (≥84,207) and unbelted occupants (96,706) sum to 109% of deaths, while 65% involve any. 1.68 counts per death; about 70% of speeding deaths also involve another factor. | factor_contributions.md Summary 1, §4; README P2-1 | `contributions/fars_overlap_summary`, `fars_overlap_combinations`, `fars_pairwise_overlap` | `analysis/contributions.py` |
| P02 | **Alcohol does not explain away speeding.** Crude exceedance RR 10.0. Alcohol accounts for 9% (7–11%) of its log-RR (4–16% by order); 69% remains after all seven blocks (RR 4.9, 4.1–5.8). Without alcohol the RR is 6.5, higher than with alcohol (3.3). E-value 7.7 for the CI limit. Speeding explains 4% of alcohol's association. | speeding_confounding.md; README P2-2 | `speeding/*` | `analysis/speeding.py`, `analysis/confounding.py` |
| P03 | **The unit matters.** Recorded alcohol: RR 4.7 for the crash being fatal, 2.7 for the driver's death, 4.3 passenger, 3.6 other-vehicle occupant, 1.22 (0.93–1.60) struck pedestrian. Exceedance × alcohol at crash level: super-additive (IC 21.0 per 1,000) but sub-multiplicative (RRR 0.49). Single-vehicle drivers: RRR 1.13 (0.84–1.53), "no departure from multiplicativity". Belt non-use dominates occupant combinations. Four-factor drug cells are withheld. | multifactor_interactions.md; README P2-3 | `multifactor/*` | `analysis/multifactor.py`, `analysis/factorial.py`, `processing/drivers.py` |
| P04 | **Drugs cannot be ranked.** Drug testing: 62% of killed vs 19% of surviving drivers. Toxicology OR between BAC .08+ and a drug is 1.23 vs 5.0 in police reports. The CRSS crude drug RR ranges from 1.7 to 73 depending only on how unknowns are allocated. In 4 high-testing states, BAC .08+ OR 2.17 vs any drug 1.28 for being the one who died. | alcohol_drugs_restraints.md §1–5; README P2-4 | `drugs/*` | `analysis/drugs.py` |
| P05 | **Belts act at a different stage.** Within-vehicle belted/unbelted RR is 0.34 whether or not the driver was speeding, and 0.32 with a BAC .08+ driver; it differs between single- and multi-vehicle crashes. Across crashes (CRSS) the unbelted RR falls from 46 to 28 to 19 as covariates are added. | alcohol_drugs_restraints.md §6; README P2-5 | `restraint_behaviour/dpc_by_driver_behaviour`, `restraint_behaviour/crss_unbelted_nested`, `matched/*` | `analysis/restraint_behaviour.py`, `analysis/matched.py` |
| P06 | **No defensible single ranking.** Exceeding the limit ranks first on adjusted risk (P ≈ 0.97). Alcohol ranks first on deaths involved and attributed burden. Predictive importance depends on the measure; crash configuration carries 47% of the explained log-likelihood. The hypothesised ranking drugs > alcohol > belts > speeding is not supported. | factor_contributions.md §2–5; README P2-6 | `contributions/ranking`, `rank_probabilities`, `predictive`, `predictive_lattice`, `nested_models`, `burden_shapley` | `analysis/contributions.py` |
| P07 | **No individual attribution.** Shares of a modelled risk change with scale and order and often need combinations that do not exist in the data. Example case (exceeded + alcohol + unbelted + night + single-vehicle): 176 drivers, 52 died; removing alcohol changes the risk by only 1.41 (0.84–2.35). | case_comparisons.md; README P2-7 | `cases/scenarios`, `cases/scenario_contrasts` | `analysis/cases.py`, `analysis/factorial.py` |

## Where the deaths are: the public website (`reports/road_deaths.md`, `website/templates/index.html`)

| ID | Finding as published | Where | Tables | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W01 | **59.6% of deaths involved at least one of four behaviours:** a driver at BAC .08+ (29.9%), a speeding-related crash (27.9%), an unbelted passenger-vehicle occupant (26.7%), an unhelmeted motorcyclist (5.3%). The shares sum to 89.8%; the overcount ratio is 1.51. | road_deaths.md Summary 1, §2; website #behaviours | `deaths/behaviour_summary`, `deaths/behaviour_overlap`, `deaths/segments` | `analysis/deaths.py` |
| W02 | **Victims of other people's behaviour.** About 11,800 deaths a year were in drunk-driving crashes, and 39.4% of those killed were not the drunk driver. 37.4% of those killed in speeding crashes were not the speeding driver. 85% of deaths in large-truck crashes were not the truck driver. | road_deaths.md Summary 2, §3, §5, §8; website key findings 1 & 3 | `deaths/victim_roles`, `deaths/segments` | `analysis/deaths.py` |
| W03 | **Drunk drivers in fatal crashes** were heavily impaired and often unlicensed but seldom had a recent conviction: 66.3% at BAC .15+, 23.7% without a valid licence, only 7.4% with a DWI conviction in the previous five years (1.8% for BAC-zero drivers). Speeding drivers: 24.0% invalid licence, 23.7% prior speeding conviction (10.0% and 16.4% for comparison drivers). | road_deaths.md Summary 3, §3, §5; website key finding 1 | `deaths/driver_records` | `analysis/deaths.py` |
| W04 | **48.6% of passenger-vehicle occupants killed with known belt use were unbelted** (56% at night, 60% in pickups). Belts would have saved up to about 6,900 a year (model-based), about 4,000 if 1 in 10 belted survivors was misrecorded and 500 if 1 in 5. | road_deaths.md Summary 4, §4; website #belts | `deaths/restraint`, `deaths/potential_lives`, `matched/belt_effect` | `analysis/deaths.py`, `analysis/matched.py` |
| W05 | **Pedestrian deaths are concentrated:** 17% of deaths; 72.6% mid-block, only 19.9% in daylight, mostly on arterials, 23.1% hit-and-run. The CRSS share of struck pedestrians who died rises from 2.2% (≤25 mph) to 33.1% (50–55 mph), adjusted RR 6.6. | road_deaths.md Summary 5, §6; website #pedestrians | `deaths/pedestrians`, `deaths/segments`, `vulnerable/pedestrian_killed_contrasts` | `analysis/deaths.py`, `analysis/vulnerable.py` |
| W06 | **Motorcycles.** Motorcyclist deaths rose 23.5% from 2019 to 2024. 37.9% of motorcyclists killed (known helmet use) wore no helmet. Helmet: 31% (20–40%) lower risk of death in rider–passenger pairs, so about 641 a year would have survived with a helmet (model-based). | road_deaths.md §7; website #motorcycles | `deaths/helmet`, `deaths/potential_lives`, `deaths/segments`, `matched/double_pair_comparison` | `analysis/deaths.py`, `analysis/matched.py` |
| W07 | **Vehicle age.** Occupants of vehicles 18+ years old had an adjusted risk of death in a police-reported crash 2.22 (1.99–2.48) times that of 0–3-year-old vehicles (1.76 with crash type and number of vehicles fixed). Their share of occupant deaths rose from 18.0% (2016) to 30.5% (2024). | road_deaths.md Summary 6, §8; website #vehicles | `person/lpv_killed_crude_vs_adjusted`, `deaths/vehicle_age` | `analysis/person.py`, `analysis/deaths.py` |
| W08 | **The 2019–21 rise was steepest for risky driving and on urban roads:** invalid-licence crashes +44.0%, hit-and-run +43%, drunk driving +33.4%, all deaths +18.9%, urban +29% vs rural +6.5%. By 2024: passenger-vehicle occupants +1.5% vs 2019, motorcyclists +23.5%, pedestrians +12.9%, cyclists +28.4%. | road_deaths.md Summary 7, §9; website #trend | `deaths/segments`, `deaths/driver_records_by_year`, `deaths/annual` | `analysis/deaths.py` |
| W09 | **States differ sharply.** The unbelted share of occupants killed ranges from about 35% to 69% by state, and the unhelmeted share of motorcyclists killed from about 4% to 74%. | road_deaths.md §10; website key finding 8, #states | `deaths/states` | `analysis/deaths.py` |
