# F06 red-team report: "Crash involvement is not crash severity" (QIE)

## 1. Target
reports/findings.md §7 (Finding 6), README finding 6. Quote: "Drivers aged 15-20 are over-represented as the culpable party (RIR 1.92, 1.87-1.97), yet crashes involving them are less severe (0.86, 0.78-0.94)"; 75+ RIR 1.65 vs 65-74 0.95; alcohol RIR 6.5 -> 2.5 across the coding break "not evidence of a change in behaviour"; police alcohol 8.5 / 10.1; charge-based 3.7; teen night 1.53 vs day 1.97.
Tables: exposure/relative_involvement, _ka, _charge_based, sample.

## 2. Reproduction
RIR_g = weighted C_g/N_g (odds of culpability in group g; exposure.py `rir_table`). Sample: 223,856 two-vehicle crashes, exactly one culpable (C1) and one non-culpable driver, hit-and-run excluded; this is 90.0% of the weighted candidate crashes (sample.csv). Script 01 reproduces: 15-20 1.916, 75+ 1.653, 65-74 0.949, 35-44 0.789 (identical to relative_involvement.csv). Script 02 reproduces police-alcohol RIR 8.51 / 10.13 (2016-18 / 2020-24 with police-known split), teen night 1.53 / day 1.97. The 0.86 is NOT from exposure.py: it is the model-A adjusted fatal-crash RR for "driver 15-20 involved" (findings.md l.144), all crash types, different population from the QIE sample.
All headline numbers reproduce.

## 4. Verdict
**SURVIVES** (confidence: high on direction of all stated numbers; medium on wording of the severity half). Serious attacks on definition, sample rule, variance, age missingness and the alcohol break all failed; the QIE age gradient is stable (teen 1.89-2.17, 75+ 1.52-1.66), the head-to-head contrast reproduces it, and a like-for-like severity check inside the QIE sample (KA 0.79) supports "involvement is not severity".

Most damaging issue: the 0.86 is a model-A-adjusted fatal RR from a different population and is not significant after adding crash circumstances (0.92, 0.84-1.01); and the 75+ RIR (1.65) hides a strong crash-type split (rear-end 1.09 vs angle 2.05). Neither changes the direction.

Also note (outside knowledge, not tested): P_CRASH2 "critical event" is assigned per vehicle, so definitions built on it overlap heavily, which is why variants 1-2 agree so closely; the only independent definitions (error codes only, charge-based) give teens higher (2.17, 2.06) and 75+ lower (1.52, 1.52).

## 5. Recommended correction
- §7: state that 0.86 is the model-A adjusted fatal-crash RR and give 0.92 (0.84-1.01) with circumstances (or the QIE-sample figure, KA 0.79); say "less severe on the crash-circumstance-free model" or soften to "not more severe".
- Add to assumptions: the 75+ RIR is ~1.1 in rear-end crashes (41% of sample) and ~2 in angle/sideswipe/head-on; the headline is a mix.
- Optionally report the head-to-head pair contrast as a pool-free check.
