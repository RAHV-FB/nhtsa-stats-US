# G03 report: model selection and recommended use (docs/model_validation.md §8, §3, §5.2)

## 1. Target
"The pre-specified rule selects boosting for both outcomes and stages; it is adequate (calibration slope 1.01–1.03 and observed/expected 1.04–1.05 for fatal …; this overall calibration nets under-prediction in crashes without a hit-and-run, O/E 1.11 (1.03–1.18), against over-prediction in hit-and-run crashes, 0.60) … better than the main-effects logistic model on every proper scoring rule (paired CIs exclude zero) and better than the other logistic variants by similar margins … gain moderate (AUROC +0.02, Brier skill +0.006 to +0.021). It does not come from the RQ2 interactions … L2 tuning and the random forest bring nothing over the simpler alternatives." (§8; tables outputs/tables/ml/*)

## 2. Reproduction
Script 01 refits (project code, 2016–21 train, 2023–24 test, fatal, circumstance stage). hgb: slope 1.028, O/E 1.036 (table: 1.028 / 1.036). Not hit-and-run O/E 1.106 (table 1.11), hit-and-run 0.604 (table 0.60). logit_main AUROC 0.9029, hgb 0.9206, paired Brier-skill difference +0.0212 (CI +0.0147 to +0.0280), all matching the committed tables. The selection rule in `select_model` matches the docstring and `selection.json`; hgb wins by 2.1–5.1% log loss and 0.020–0.026 AUROC against tolerances of 1% and 0.005. In my 2022 bootstrap (100 reps, script 02) no replicate lets any logistic variant pass the rule. Selection was made on 2022 only. Headline numbers reproduce.

## 4. Verdict: WEAKENED (confidence: medium)
What stands: the rule was applied as written, on 2022 only; boosting beats the main-effects and interaction logit on every proper score with CIs excluding zero; the RQ2 interactions add essentially nothing (+0.0014 AUROC); L2 adds nothing; overall calibration and the subgroup O/E numbers reproduce.

What does not: (i) the implied reading that boosting's value is flexible structure beyond what a transparent model can give is overstated. A plain logistic model with crash-type interactions captures 73% of the AUROC gain, and its Brier-skill deficit to boosting is +0.0031 (CI −0.0007 to +0.0082), compatible with zero, while §8 rates the remaining gain as "moderate" and says the logit models are "nearly as good" without that comparison; §7 admits it was not run. (ii) "pre-specified" is supported only by a code docstring; the plan and change log do not contain the rule. (iii) The explanation of the hit-and-run miss through "proxy unknowns" is contradicted by O/E 0.52 in hit-and-run crashes without any unknown proxy, and the 0.76 in the training years shows it is not just test-year drift. (iv) "RF brings nothing" ignores RF's significant AUROC gain (+0.009 to +0.024) and rests on one untuned fit.

Most damaging issue: attack 1. The recommendation (boosting for stratification, logit for inference) is defensible, but the key interpretation, that crash-type-specific structure is where boosting's gain lies, was left unquantified although it is cheap to quantify, and the quantification shows a transparent model gets most of the way.

## 5. Recommended correction
- §8: add "a logistic model with crash-type interaction terms (not pre-specified; evaluated post hoc) recovers about three quarters of the AUROC gain; boosting's remaining Brier-skill advantage is +0.003 (−0.001 to +0.008)".
- Say "pre-specified in the module docstring" or add the rule to the plan's change log with its date.
- Replace the "proxy unknowns" explanation of hit-and-run (§5.2) with "unexplained; also present in training years (O/E 0.76), and in crashes without proxy unknowns (0.52)".
- Qualify "random forest brings nothing": "brings no calibration or scoring-rule gain; AUROC gain +0.01 to +0.02; one untuned configuration".
