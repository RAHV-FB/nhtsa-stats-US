## 3 Overall assessment
MINOR ISSUES, bordering on MATERIAL (confidence medium; limited by the untested items listed in section 1). Disclosure is generally good. The undisclosed items change magnitudes, not directions.

Most damaging bias: the mixed-source numerator and denominator behind the "frequency is not danger" headline (issue 1). It pairs FARS analyst-coded speeding (27.2% of fatal crashes) with a CRSS police-coded denominator (CRSS fatal share 20.4%). Reading both from CRSS gives x8.6, not x12.8, for exceeding the limit. The project's own adjusted CRSS-only RR is 5.22. The bias is a known measurement gap, but it is carried into the headline number. The next most important item is the one-sided belt sensitivity (issue 4). Hindsight coding of killed occupants as unbelted (90% of the ejected, vs 36% of the non-ejected) can lower the 65.8% as much as survivor misreport does.

## 4 Recommended corrections
1. Show the CRSS-only ratio (x8.6) beside x12.8, or drop the mixed ratio for speeding.
2. Add a killed-side misclassification scenario (killed "none" that were truly belted) to the belt sensitivity grid, and state that 65.8% is an upper-end estimate.
3. Give the measured-only bound (27%) next to the 39.4% for W02, and note that about 38% of drunk drivers in W03 are imputed. State that measured-only prior DWI is 9.5%.
4. Report the pedestrian fatal shares after correcting for the missing speed limit (about 1.9% to 29%), or flag them as inflated by outcome-dependent missingness.
5. Split the "speeding-related" 27.9% into exceeded or racing (11.3%) and the rest in the headline.
