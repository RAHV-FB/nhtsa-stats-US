# F07 red-team report: within-vehicle belt effect

## 1. Target
"Within the same vehicle, unbelted occupants die far more often than belted ones." findings.md §8 / README 7: DPC (driver + right-front pairs, 45,805 pairs with >=1 death) belted risk 65.8% (64.5-67.0%) lower; clogit OR 5.07 (4.84-5.31); misreporting 37.9% at 10%, 4.4% at 20%, vanishing at ~21%, "the direction is not [uncertain], unless misreporting is very large"; frailty 2.81 / 1.03; helmets RR 0.69; CRSS RR 46.2 / 24.0 "not belt effectiveness".

## 2. Reproduction
scripts/audit/red_team/F07/01_reproduce_and_attacks.py (via project functions matched.pair_frame / dpc_combined / dpc_tipping_point on fars_person): 90,240 pairs, 45,805 with a death, reduction 0.6585, tipping f 0.2117; f=0.10 -> 0.3789; f=0.20 -> 0.0436. All match the tables. Estimator: inverse-variance mean of 4 log-RRs of death counts (driver/other as subject, other's belt fixed); only survivors coded belted are moved to unbelted under misreporting; deaths keep their code. Pairs need exactly one eligible driver and one RF occupant with known restraint, age>=13.

## 4. Verdict: SURVIVES, with a wording downgrade to WEAKENED on the misreporting paragraph only. Overall: SURVIVES (confidence medium-high).
Direction and rough size of the within-vehicle reduction are robust: reproduces exactly, estimator recovers truth in simulation, stable across years, age, alcohol, speeding, and unknown-restraint coding (61-74% for plausible recodings). The most damaging issue is the misreporting sensitivity: it is one-sided (survivors only), applied uniformly, and the "vanishing at 21%" and "direction not uncertain" statements are pooled-average claims. Allowing errors in the other direction (unbelted-coded deaths that were belted, h=5%) cuts the 10% scenario from 37.9% to 28.2% and the vanishing point below 20%; within multi-vehicle angle crashes it vanishes at ~10%. No outside source says what f actually is; the data cannot identify it.

## 5. Recommended correction
Add one sentence to findings.md §8 and docs/limitations.md: misreporting scenarios are one-sided and pooled; reverse misclassification of deaths, and crash-type heterogeneity (angle crashes ~51%, rollovers ~77-84%), mean the 65.8% is an average over fatal-crash mix, and 21% is not a threshold for every crash type. Report bounds for unknown restraint (61-74% plausible recodings). Optional: add "direction is not uncertain unless misreporting is large (here roughly 10-20% of belted survivors, less in some crash types)".
