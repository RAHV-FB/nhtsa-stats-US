# P01 red-team report: "Official factor counts overlap and cannot be added"

## 1. Target
"These four counts sum to 109% of all deaths, but only 65% of deaths involve any of them: each death in this group is counted 1.68 times on average. About 70% of speeding-related deaths also involve one of the other three factors." Source: reports/factor_contributions.md Summary 1 and §4; README Phase-2 finding 1 (README says "65% of deaths involve any of them"). Tables: contributions/fars_overlap_summary, fars_overlap_combinations, fars_pairwise_overlap.

## 2. Reproduction
Method read from `contributions.fars_overlap()`. Deaths are killed persons in FARS. Speeding is the crash-level `speeding` flag. BAC>=.08 is the max over drivers per MI implicate, with null set to 0. Drug is "yes" if any driver is positive, "no" only if every driver is determinate, otherwise "undetermined"; undetermined counts as not-positive. Unbelted is the victim's own `restraint=="none"` with PER_TYP 1, 2 or 9. Alcohol is averaged as expected counts across implicates. Because the other three factors are fixed within a cell, that expectation equals the mean of the per-implicate unions, so the method is exact.

My independent per-implicate computation (`scripts/audit/red_team/P01/01_reproduce_attacks.py`) gives sum = 109.0%, union = 64.8%, ratio = 1.683, BAC deaths = 105,821 and 70.1% of speeding deaths with another factor. All match the committed tables. The union moves only 0.6468 to 0.6484 across the ten implicates, so imputation variance is negligible.

## 4. Verdict: WEAKENED (confidence: high on mechanics, medium on size)
The qualitative claim, that the counts overlap and cannot be added, is robust. The 109% sum is a lower bound because the drug count is. The union (65%) and the speeding-with-other share (70%) are also lower bounds, because they assume undetermined-drug crashes are negative. The 1.68 ratio can move either way: it is 1.47 without drugs and 1.80–1.96 under the other drug-handling scenarios in this report. The words "only 65%" read as a finding, but with 63% of deaths in undetermined-drug crashes the union could be anywhere up to 95% (rigorous bound), or about 83–84% under a MAR scenario. Verified: the "at least" qualifier applies only to the drug count, not the sentence's totals.

**Single most damaging issue:** the headline union and ratio are presented as measured quantities ("only 65%", "1.68 times") although they depend on the 63% of deaths whose drug status is undetermined being scored negative. The sentence also lacks a qualifier for the 8% unknown restraint and for the crash-level versus victim-level mixing in the pooled ratio.

## 5. Recommended correction
- Rewrite as "sum to *at least* 109%, while *at least* 65% (a lower bound, since drug status is undetermined for 63% of deaths) involve any of them".
- Report the three-factor figure with fully measured data (sum 85%, union 58%, ratio 1.47) beside the four-factor one, or give a bounds range for union (65–95%) and ratio (1.47 to 1.80).
- State "about 70%" as "at least 70%".
- Stratify by occupant versus non-occupant (ratio 1.73 vs 1.14), or restrict the unbelted comparison to occupant deaths.
- Add the lower-bound language to the README finding and an entry in docs/limitations.md.
