# W09 red-team report: "States differ sharply" (unbelted 35-69%, unhelmeted 4-74%)

## 1. Target
Website key finding 8 / #states: "The unbelted share of occupants killed ranges from 35% to 69% by state, and the share of motorcyclists killed without a helmet from 4% to 74%." Sources: `website/templates/index.html` (key finding 8, #states and its note), `reports/templates/road_deaths.md` §10-§11, `docs/limitations.md` §8. Table `outputs/tables/deaths/states.csv`, code `deaths.states_table`.

## 2. Reproduction (scripts/audit/red_team/W09/01_repro_bounds.py)
Estimand (from `deaths.py`): unbelted = A_RESTUSE==2 over passenger-vehicle occupants killed (A_BODY 1-5) with A_RESTUSE in {1,2}; unhelmeted = A_HELMUSE==2 over motorcyclists killed (A_BODY 7) with A_HELMUSE in {1,2}. Note the site text says "occupants", but the base is passenger-vehicle occupants only. Unknowns are excluded from the denominator. These shares are not multiply imputed, so the MI averaging is irrelevant here.
My independent recomputation from interim FARS reproduces the published extremes:
- Unbelted: min OR 34.6% (FIPS 41), max NH 69.4% (FIPS 33). The table gives the same values (0.346, 0.694).
- Unhelmeted: min WA 3.5%, max ND 74.0% (FIPS 38), next IN 73.0% and IA 72.8%. The table gives the same values.
Rounded as published: 35-69% and 4-74%. Reproduced.

## 4. Verdict
SURVIVES (high confidence on the numbers, medium on the wording). The claim is a descriptive range. I reproduced both ranges to the percentage point, and none of the attacks narrowed the extremes: they hold under worst-case treatment of unknowns, they are much wider than sampling noise, they persist across 3-year windows, and standardising for rural/age/sex leaves them unchanged. The documented caveats (unknown belt shares, recording practice, not rates) are in the site note, limitations §8 and the report.

Most damaging issue (minor): the headline sentence is a point range of two extremes with no uncertainty. The ND 74% helmet maximum rests on 131 known riders (CI about 66-81%) and the middle ranks are sensitive to unknowns (up to 27% for DC belts, 20% for WY helmets). The extremes do not depend on this, but the ranking and the reader's sense of "35% vs 69%" depend on definitions the key finding sentence does not state.

## 5. Recommended corrections
- Key finding 8: say "passenger-vehicle occupants killed whose belt use was recorded" and "motorcyclists killed whose helmet use was recorded", and add "shares of deaths, not belt-use rates".
- Extend the #states note with the helmet-unknown range (0% to 20% by state).
- Optionally mark the 2019->2024 change as unreliable for states with <60 deaths in 2019 (DC: 23 -> 47; CI +22% to +253%), as is done for share bases <50.
- road_deaths.md §10 should repeat the belt-unknown caveat that the website and limitations §8 carry.
