# F08 red-team report: "The post-2020 rise in crash severity is only partly explained by the recorded crash mix"

## 1. Target
reports/findings.md §9 (Finding 8) and README finding 8. Fatal share 0.51% to 0.64%, RR 1.25 (1.14-1.37). "The fall in reported crashes accounts for 54% (29-78%) of the rise". Composition "accounts for between 28% and 49%" (49% ref 2016-19 model, 37% ref 2020-24). Within-circumstance RR 1.11 (1.04-1.20), "clearest in speeding-related crashes (1.38)". Tables: outputs/tables/trends/*.

## 2. Reproduction (script 01, committed tables)
- Denominator 54% = -ln(0.8869)/ln(1.2508) = 0.536 (LOG scale; numerator_denominator.csv). Composition 49% / 37% = (p01-p0)/(p1-p0) and (p1-p10)/(p1-p0) on the PROBABILITY (pp) scale; re-run gives 0.487 / 0.373. The same split on the log scale is 0.515 / 0.347 (script 01).
- The two headline "shares" therefore use different scales. The composition split is not sensitive to that choice (49 vs 52). The denominator split is moderately sensitive: pp scale gives 56% (numerator first) or 51% (denominator first) (script 01).

## 4. Verdict: WEAKENED (confidence: medium-high)
The direction stands: fatal share up ~25%, roughly half a ratio-accounting from fewer reported crashes, a residual within-circumstance rise of ~1.1 that survives every variant I ran (RR 1.08-1.18). What is overstated is the precision of the composition range and the 54% presented without its complete-case counterpart.

Most damaging issue: "Composition accounts for between 28% and 49%" is presented as the reference-dependence range, but it depends on covariate choice and period choice far more than the stated range allows. 25% (drop imputed alcohol and the three drifting variables), 60% (other baselines), and 6% (reference 2020-24 model vs 2022-24 comparison) all fall outside it. The largest single contributor (surface) is a coding-drift variable, and imputed alcohol, which the project itself flags as possibly severity-informed, supplies another fifth of the composition effect.

## 5. Recommended correction
- Replace "between 28% and 49%" by "roughly a quarter to a half (about 25-60% across covariate sets and baseline years), and with an interval that includes 0 when the 2020-24 model is the reference (37%, -3 to 77%)".
- Report the denominator share under both outcomes: "54% (imputed), 46% (complete case)"; say that 29-78% is conservative because the CRSS numerator equals FARS by calibration.
- State that the denominator and composition splits overlap (fewer minor crashes also changes the mix), and that "accounts for" is arithmetic, not mechanism.
- Add one sentence: recorded speeding rose among fatal crashes (x1.30) but not among non-fatal ones, so the speeding subgroup result is a restatement of that, which could be behaviour or recording.
- Fix 1.11 vs 1.13 comparability when contrasting with 1.38.
