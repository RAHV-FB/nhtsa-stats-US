# W04 red-team report: unbelted share and lives belts would have saved

## 1. Target
Website key finding 2 and #belts, and road_deaths.md Summary 4 and §4: 48.6% of occupants killed with known belt use were unbelted. "Up to about 6,900 a year" would have survived if belted; about 4,000 under 10% misreporting and about 500 under 20%. Tables: deaths/restraint, deaths/potential_lives, matched/belt_effect.

## 2. Reproduction (scripts 01, 03)
- A_RESTUSE codes: 1 = used (includes 1,879 child-restraint deaths), 2 = not used, 3 = unknown.
- Killed passenger-vehicle occupants: 215,331 (published 215,277). Known: 194,328 (published 194,279). Unbelted: 94,416 (published 94,400).
- Unbelted share 48.586%, matching the published 48.59%. Lives per year = 94,416 x 0.658495 / 9 = 6,908, against the published 6,907.
- The headline numbers reproduce. Differences are about 50 rows.
- Lives = N_unbelted x (1 - RR). It is Evans' standard formula. Low/high only carry the DPC bootstrap CI of the effectiveness (6,769–7,027), about +/-2%.

## 4. Verdict: SURVIVES with wording issues (medium-high confidence)
The numbers reproduce, and the rear-seat transport test supports applying the effectiveness beyond the front seat. The weaknesses are interpretive.
- "Up to about 6,900" reads as a ceiling, but excluding the 21,003 deaths with unknown belt use understates the as-recorded estimate. The ejection-based imputation gives about 7,500/yr.
- The as-recorded 65.8% is probably inflated by survivor misreporting; the 5% scenario (52.5%) sits nearer published values (outside knowledge).
- The 10% and 20% scenarios give 4,000 and 500, so the ranges are very wide. This is stated.

Most damaging issues: (a) 60.7% of the unbelted deaths are in single-occupant vehicles, outside the pair-based estimator's support; (b) the "up to" wording combined with the unknown-restraint exclusion. The effect is about +9% under imputation and up to +22% under the all-unbelted bound.

## 5. Recommended correction
- Replace "up to about 6,900" with "about 6,900 as recorded, before allowing for deaths with unknown belt use".
- Add a lives bound for unknown-belt deaths, or state that they are excluded.
- Note that the 5% scenario is closest to published effectiveness.
- Say that the low/high columns reflect sampling error only.
