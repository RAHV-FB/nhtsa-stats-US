# F03 red-team report: "Speeding is two things"

## 1. Target
"Crashes in which a driver exceeded the posted limit were by far the most likely to be fatal; crashes coded 'too fast for conditions' showed no detectable increase" (website #speed; README 3; reports/findings.md §4). Published: exceeded/racing adj. fatal RR 5.22 (4.34-6.27), crude 10.03; too fast for conditions (TF) 1.06 (0.89-1.26), crude 1.34; KA 1.18 (1.03-1.36). Tables: severity/fatal_speeding_type, ka_speeding_type, measurement/qba_grid, burden/attributable_fractions_fatal.

## 2. Reproduction
Derivation (read from code): CRSS/FARS vehicle SPEEDREL 2/3 = exceeded/racing, 4 = TF, 5 = unspecified, 0 = no, other = unknown (harmonize.speeding_type). Crash level is "any vehicle", with precedence exceeded > unspecified > TF > unknown > no (features.py L228-240). So the type belongs to the crash, not to the driver who was killed.
Script 01 (`scripts/audit/red_team/F03/01_variants.py`) reproduced 5.22 (4.34-6.27) and 1.06 (0.89-1.26) exactly. Script 02 reproduced crude RR 10.03 and 1.34.

## 4. Verdict
WEAKENED, confidence medium-high. The strong claim survives: exceeded/racing is a large association under every variant (3.4 to 6.2, always CI above 3). The data support the direction of the contrast, and the QBA for exceedance holds up (my differential-specificity extension did not break it).
The weak claim is overstated. "No detectable increase" for TF depends on the adjustment set (0.97 / 1.06 / 1.29 across A-without-surface / A / B). It is sign-heterogeneous by speed limit and unprotected by any bias analysis. Its CI cannot rule out meaningful effects, and KA is significant at 1.18. Most damaging single issue: the TF null is exposed to the same post-crash, severity-dependent recording problem as exceedance, yet only exceedance received a QBA. The published QBA direction (fatal recorded better) pushes toward the null, whereas the FARS comparison (CRSS under-records speeding in fatal crashes) pushes TF upward.

## 5. Recommended correction
Replace "no detectable increase / none detected" with "a small or no association (adjusted RR 1.06, CI 0.89-1.26 in model A; 1.29 in model B; 1.18 for fatal-or-serious), not separable from a modest excess". Report model B for the type contrast and the speed-limit heterogeneity. Add a QBA for TF. Explain or drop the NaN sp=0.99 rows. Keep the "exceeded is the large association" claim, and say it is 3.4-6.2 depending on specification and population. Say that the automated-enforcement relevance rests on association only: crude, with enforcement effects not estimated here.
