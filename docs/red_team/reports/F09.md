# F09 red-team report: measurement by severity (alcohol testing, unknown shares, belts, drugs/distraction)

## 1. Target
"Police-recorded behaviours are measured differently by severity. Drug and distraction fields cannot support severity or trend analysis at all." (reports/findings.md §10 Finding 9; README finding 9, which says "recorded more completely in severe crashes"; docs/limitations.md §2 first bullet; findings §4 "recording is therefore more complete when crashes are investigated more thoroughly"). Tables: outputs/tables/measurement/*.

## 2. Reproduction
Reproduced (scripts/audit/red_team/F09/01, 03, 04):
- Alcohol-tested share, killed vs survived: 73.7% vs 28.5% (2016), 65.7% vs 23.1% (2023). Killed test-status unknown 4.8% to 13.9%. Matches fars_testing.
- Police "DRINKING" vs BAC>=.08 among tested: sensitivity 0.871 (killed), 0.982 (survived); matches police_vs_bac. These are sensitivities, with PPV 0.835 and 0.711.
- Belts: CRSS 98.7% (uninjured) and 50.8% (killed), from restraint_reporting.csv.
- Speeding in fatal crashes: CRSS 20.4% (imputed-fatal; table row "all") or 21.0% (19.0-23.1, observed-K, script 03) vs FARS 27.2-27.3%.
- Unknown shares in unknown_by_year.csv (alcohol non-fatal 11.3% in 2018 to 30.2% in 2019; drugs 15.9% in 2018 to 58.0% in 2020) are as published.

## 4. Verdict
**WEAKENED** (confidence: medium-high). The measurable facts (testing rates, unknown shares and breaks, belt gradient, 87/98, speeding gap) all reproduce and are direction-robust. Two parts of the framing are not supported:
1. The headline sentence "recorded more completely in severe crashes" (README 9, limitations §2). Within CRSS, police-recorded alcohol, drug and distraction are *more often unknown* in fatal crashes (by 10-20 pp, significant for alcohol, drugs and distraction).
2. "Cannot support severity or trend analysis at all". Post-2020 the unknown share is flat, and a descriptive known-status contrast exists. The correct statement is that severity contrasts are not identified without an assumption (bounds overlap).

The most damaging issue is (1). It states the opposite of the project's own `unknown_by_severity` table for police-reported fields. Only toxicology testing and FARS-level speeding ascertainment are more complete for severe crashes.

## 5. Recommended correction
- README 9 / limitations §2: replace with "Recording is differential by severity: toxicology testing is far more common in killed drivers, whereas police-recorded alcohol, drug and distraction fields are more often *unknown* in fatal CRSS crashes (alcohol 56% vs 37% in 2020-24)."
- Finding 9: add "the 98% is among suspicion-selected tested survivors, so 87% vs 98% is not a like-for-like accuracy comparison" and report PPV (84% / 71%) beside sensitivity.
- Replace "cannot ... at all" with "cannot identify a severity association or a trend across 2016-2020 without assumptions; bounds overlap (drugs 2020-24)".
- Restraint: state that protection and over-reporting are not separable here. FARS killed (51%) agrees with CRSS killed (50.8%).
