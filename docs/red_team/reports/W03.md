# W03 red-team report: drunk (and speeding) drivers' records

## 1. Target
"Drunk drivers in fatal crashes ... 66.3% were at .15 or more and 23.7% had no valid licence, but only 7.4% had a DWI conviction on record from the previous five years" (road_deaths.md Summary 3, §3; website key finding 1). BAC-zero comparison: 11.6% invalid licence, 1.8% prior DWI. §3 adds: "this share is a lower bound on prior impaired driving. Even so, most drunk drivers ... had no recent conviction for programmes aimed at convicted drivers to act on." §5: speeding drivers 24.0% invalid licence and 23.7% prior speeding conviction, against 10.0% and 16.4% for drivers neither speeding nor at any BAC. Source: `deaths/driver_records` (and `driver_records_by_year`), `analysis/deaths.py`.

## 2. Reproduction
Estimand: FARS drivers (PER_TYP 1, n=497,955), "drunk" = MI BAC >= .08. Each share is a ratio of sums within one implicate (known status only), then averaged over the 10 implicates. Unknown codes are excluded from the base.
`01_repro_and_attacks.py` gives .15+ = 0.6631, invalid licence = 0.2366 and prior DWI = 0.0744. These match the table (0.663057, 0.236601, 0.0744307). Reproduced.
Speeding and "neither" (`04`): invalid licence 0.2399 vs 0.0998, prior speeding 0.2372 vs 0.1642. Reproduced.

## 4. Verdict: SURVIVES for the direction, WEAKENED in magnitude and wording (medium-high confidence)
Every attack was run on the data, and none reverses the qualitative claim. A prior DWI conviction is under 20% of drunk drivers under any measured definition or national bound. The 66.3% and 23.7% are robust: the measured-only values are 70.1% and 25.7%, and the implicate range is narrow.

I rate it WEAKENED rather than SURVIVES on two grounds. (a) The 7.4% is diluted by MI misclassification. In the 59.5% of the group with a measured BAC the share is 9.5%, and it is 10.5% at measured .15+. In the 40.5% with an imputed BAC it is only 4.2%. The "7%" headline is therefore a modelled value that understates the measured-subgroup value by about 2 points. (b) The national figure hides a spread of roughly 2% to 20% across states (attack 5). The policy sentence, "most ... had no recent conviction for programmes aimed at convicted drivers to act on", has an unexamined base: roughly a third had a DWI, an invalid licence or a suspension on record (attack 6).

Most damaging issue: attack 2. Imputation dilutes the DWI share, and the report presents "7.4%" without saying that 40.5% of the group is imputed-only and that the measured group sits near 9.5%.

## 5. Recommended correction
- Add a measured-BAC sensitivity row to `driver_records` (BAC .08+ from ALC_RES alone: 59.5% of the group, DWI about 9.5%, .15+ about 70%, invalid licence about 25.7%).
- Reword to "about 7 to 10 per cent" or "fewer than one in ten".
- Add to limitations.md §8 a note on state heterogeneity in record completeness (1.6% to 20.1%).
- Qualify the policy sentence: it applies only to DWI-conviction-based programmes, and about one drunk driver in three had some adverse record (2018+).

## 6. Scripts (scripts/audit/red_team/W03/)
- `01_repro_and_attacks.py`: reproduces the three headline numbers; computes unknown-code bounds, age standardisation, state spread, BAC-band DWI and 2018+ broader-record shares. Its section A uses a wrong ALC_RES scale and is superseded by `03`.
- `02_alc_res_coding.py`: establishes the ALC_RES coding (thousandths; 995-999 are no-test or unknown codes).
- `03_measured_vs_imputed.py`: measured vs imputed split of the .08+ group; per-implicate .15+ range.
- `04_measured_comparison.py`: measured-BAC bands; speeding comparisons; broader record.

Notes: ALC_RES interpretation (0-940 measured; 995+ not tested or unknown) is inferred from the data (P1 = ALC_RES // 10 for measured values), not read from a codebook. The 2018+ suspension fields are coded from 2018 only. I did not check whether PREV_DWI's look-back window is the same in every state.
