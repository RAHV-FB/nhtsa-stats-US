# F01 red-team report: "Frequency is not danger"

## 1. Target
reports/findings.md section 2 (Finding 1) and README finding 1: pedestrian crashes are 1.2% of police-reported crashes (CRSS, weighted) but 18.6% of fatal crashes (FARS census); over-representation x15.6 (13.2-18.5). Exceeding the limit/racing x12.8, motorcycle x9.5. Wet surface x0.85, rain x0.77, intersection x0.58, driver 15-20 x0.65 are "under-represented". A Robustness paragraph says CRSS and FARS fatal compositions agree for darkness, pedestrians and single-vehicle crashes (supporting the mixing of the two systems) but not for motorcycles or young drivers. Tables: descriptive/frequency_vs_severity, measurement/fars_crss_fatal_composition.

## 2. Reproduction
Yes (scripts/audit/red_team/F01/01_reproduce_and_mix.py, own computation from processed data). Pedestrian: CRSS share 0.0119, FARS share 0.1858, ratio 15.60. Exceeded limit 12.79, motorcycle 9.54, young driver 0.649, intersection 0.580, wet 0.848, rain 0.771. These match the committed table. The estimand is the FARS fatal share divided by the CRSS weighted share. The CI uses the CRSS denominator SE only (descriptive.py lines 158-165; FARS treated as a census).

## 4. Verdict: WEAKENED (confidence: medium-high)
The qualitative claim survives every attack. Pedestrians and motorcycles are massively over-represented. Exceeding the limit is strongly over-represented. Wet surface, rain, intersections and teen drivers are under-represented, and in the CRSS-internal ratio the CIs for all four exclude 1. The headline pedestrian number is reproduced and is moderately sensitive: 15.6 vs 14.3 internally.

Most damaging issue: the x12.8 for "exceeded limit / racing" mixes a FARS numerator (10.9% of fatal crashes) with a CRSS denominator (0.85%) when the two systems demonstrably record police speeding differently. Within CRSS the ratio is 8.6 (6.5-11.4), about a third lower, and outside the published CI of 10.4-15.7. The report's own Robustness paragraph concedes the speeding mismatch, but the table row is presented with the same confidence as the others. Motorcycle (9.5 vs 8.5) is borderline. The published CIs convey only denominator sampling error and understate cross-system uncertainty.

## 5. Recommended correction
- Add a CRSS-internal over-representation column (or CI) next to the mixed ratio. Report the speeding row as "about 9-13" and the motorcycle row as "about 8.5-9.5".
- State the pedestrian figure as "about 14-16".
- State beside the table that the CI reflects CRSS denominator sampling error only.
- Explicitly mark the speeding and motorcycle rows as cross-system-sensitive.
- Note that CRSS weights are controlled to FARS fatal totals, so agreement at the totals level is partly by construction.
