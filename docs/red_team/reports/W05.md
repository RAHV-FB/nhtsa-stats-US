## 4. Verdict: SURVIVES (confidence: high on the FARS descriptive shares, medium-high on the CRSS gradient)
Every number reproduced exactly; alternative location sources, hit-and-run definitions and model specifications keep the claims within a few points or inside the published CI. Darkness is not merely exposure: it is 2x over-represented in deaths relative to struck pedestrians. Nothing amounts to REFUTED or material WEAKENED.

Most damaging issue (minor): 29% of the CRSS struck pedestrians have no usable posted speed limit (31% urban, vs 5.5% of FARS deaths), the group has a conspicuously low fatality rate (3.1%), and this missingness is nowhere disclosed. It does not overturn the gradient (RR 7.2 if excluded) but the 2.2% and 33.1% crude rates are computed with that group in the model, and the site says nothing about it. Second: pooling hit-and-run to "23%" hides a rise from about 20.5% (2016-19) to about 25% (2020-24).

## 5. Recommended corrections
- Add one sentence to §6 / limitations.md §8: "29% of struck pedestrians in CRSS have an unreported posted limit; excluding them, the 50-55 vs <=25 RR is 7.2 rather than 6.6."
- Either say "arterial roads (62% principal or minor)" in place of "major urban roads", or leave; optionally state hit-and-run was about 21% before 2020 and about 25% since.
- Optionally note the RR is 7.4 in 2016-19 and 5.8 in 2020-24 (CIs overlap; no formal test of change was run by me).
