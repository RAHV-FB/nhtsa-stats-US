Alcohol RR for the crash being fatal 4.7, driver death 2.7, passenger 4.3, other-vehicle occupant 3.6, struck pedestrian 1.22 (0.93-1.60); crash-level exceeded x alcohol RRR 0.49; single-vehicle RRR 1.13 (0.84-1.53) "no departure from multiplicativity". ## 2. Reproduction

All 24,651 pedestrians with known outcome are linked to a striking vehicle (linked share 1.0 in both killed and not killed), so the linkage does not select on the outcome.

## 4. Verdict: WEAKENED (confidence medium)
The directional finding survives: a struck pedestrian's death is far less associated with the striking driver's alcohol than an occupant's death is, under every specification tried (pedestrian 1.2 to 2.2 against 4 to 13 for occupants). The pedestrian number reproduces and survives adding the pedestrian's own alcohol. The cross-unit magnitudes and their ordering do not survive harmonisation. The most damaging issue is attack 1. The headline set "4.7 / 2.7 / 4.3 / 3.6" is presented as the same exposure measured against different victims, but the sets are adjusted differently. Under a common specification the driver RR is 6.2 and the passenger RR 8.8, so the driver-versus-passenger gap is a modelling artefact. The pedestrian result also depends on adjusting for light and time of day, and its size is presented on the relative scale only (RD 1.78 pp is the largest of any unit).

## 5. Recommended correction
- Add a harmonised-specification row to victims.csv.
- State that the RRs are not comparable across units without a common adjustment set (belt use and the other driver's behaviour change the driver and passenger RRs by about 2x).
- Qualify "much more weakly associated" with "in relative terms", and show the risk difference.
- Reword "responsible driver" for the passenger and other-vehicle units.
- Add a power or equivalence statement to the single-vehicle "no departure from multiplicativity" claim.
