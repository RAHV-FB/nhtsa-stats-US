# F10 red-team report: Burden and interventions

## 1. Target
"Speeding-related 9.1% (6.9-11.3%) ~ 3,736 deaths/yr; alcohol 19.7% (16.6-22.8%) ~ 8,072/yr; joint 26.5% ... Under plausible under-recording of speeding in non-fatal crashes, the speeding fraction shrinks towards zero; the alcohol fraction does not (burden/bias_sensitivity)." "The database identifies no intervention effect." Source: reports/findings.md section 11; burden/attributable_fractions_fatal, burden/bias_sensitivity, policy/evidence_ledger.

## 2. Reproduction
Estimand (burden.py): g-computation on survey-weighted logistic model A (PRE_CRASH, CRSS 2016-2024 pooled, YEAR as covariate), AF = (obs - counterfactual)/obs over CRSS crashes; deaths = AF x FARS mean deaths/yr 2022-24 (41,000), assuming averted fatal crashes carry average deaths/crash. Reproduced with 01_af_variants.py: speeding 0.0911 (0.0693-0.1129), 3,736; alcohol 0.1969 (0.1660-0.2277), 8,072. Identical to the table.

## 4. Verdict
**WEAKENED** (confidence: medium-high). The direction survives: alcohol is the largest single severity-pathway share, the joint fraction is larger than either, and the report states it is not lives saved and that no intervention effect is identified (attack 8 failed to break that). The "no intervention effect" claim survives. What is overstated is the precision and the one-sided sensitivity story.

Most damaging issue: the headline fractions depend on analysis choices that move them far beyond the published CIs, and the choices that move the speeding fraction up (2022-24 window 11.9%, FARS prevalence 12.6%) are not shown, while only the downward "shrinks to zero" reading is highlighted. For alcohol, using NHTSA-imputed alcohol, which the project itself flags as possibly severity-informed, gives 19.7%; the police-reported coding gives 12.7-15.1% (a lower bound). So "alcohol does not shrink" is true for the grid, not for the coding.

## 5. Recommended correction
- Report the fractions as ranges across window and alcohol coding (speeding ~6-12%, alcohol ~13-22%), not 9.1 +/- 2.2 and 19.7 +/- 3.1. State that the CI is sampling-only.
- Add a period-specific AF (2022-24 model) next to the pooled one, since scaling uses 2022-24 deaths.
- Reword the bias sentence to: "speeding is sensitive to under-recording in non-fatal crashes (and could be higher if fatal crashes under-record it, as the FARS comparison suggests); alcohol is less sensitive to under-recording but depends on the imputed coding."
- Drop "robust to plausible differential ascertainment" in the ledger, or define "plausible".
- Reconcile 8.9/19.4/25.7 with 9.1/19.7/26.5.
